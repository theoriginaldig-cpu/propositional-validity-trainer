How this works

P₁, P₂, Q and R stand for any statements of PROP. A general claim holds only if it is true whatever statements they are.

To show a claim holds, reason about an arbitrary valuation v. For example, to show P₁ ⊨ Q, take any valuation on which P₁ is true and show that Q must be true on it. In the proof puzzles, you put the steps of such an argument in order. At Medium and Hard, some of the steps on offer are invalid and must be left out.

To show a claim fails, choose particular statements for the letters, built from elementary statements A, B and C, for which the claim comes out false. Then say which validity claims hold for your choice, and give a valuation for each one that fails. You can type ~ or - for ¬, & for ∧, v or | for ∨, and -> or > for →, or use the buttons. Outer brackets may be left off.

The same reasoning works in any logic that extends classical propositional logic, with “model” in place of “valuation”.

Questions are generated at random. Every verdict is computed exactly, and every model answer is checked before it is shown.
